# Hostile questions and evidence-based responses

## “You observed only ten referrals. Why should we fund anything?”

Ten cases are not enough to promise savings, which is why the 6.3–8.1 minute estimate is labeled projected and the pilot begins with a two-week measured baseline. The case for a pilot also uses independent volume evidence: 5,800 reported referrals per month and a reported 62% fax share. The pilot is designed to resolve the uncertainty cheaply, not to pretend it is gone. If the measured baseline or 400-case value gate fails, the kill criteria stop the work.

## “Why not automate prior authorization, where the burden looks larger?”

Prior authorization has larger projected burden, but lower near-term feasibility. Most payer portals have no API, phone chases are material, the “authorization existed but was not attached” rate is unknown, and Caregate write access takes 8–12 reported weeks. Referral intake has a one-day reported RightFax API path and supports a read-only assisted design. We should earn the right to take on the harder workflow after proving governance and evaluation discipline.

## “The denial numbers conflict. Doesn’t that invalidate the discovery?”

The conflict is itself a high-value finding. The measured 3,100 worked denials, projected 5,781 first-pass denials, and staff-reported 15–20 cases per day cannot all describe the same population and period without additional definitions. That is why the recommendation is a deterministic reconciliation dashboard before an AI application. We are refusing to attach a savings claim to an incoherent baseline.

## “Why can’t staff just use ChatGPT if half of them already do?”

The survey measured 51% work AI use, and 71% of those users reported personal accounts, while only 6 respondents reported formal training. Kestrel has no AI policy and no BAA with a general AI vendor. Referral documents can contain PHI, so consumer-account experimentation is not an acceptable production path. The pilot requires an approved data flow, auditability, retention rules, and human verification.

## “Isn’t a 99.5% precision threshold arbitrary?”

It is a proposed decision threshold, not an observed capability claim. Critical identity and destination fields have asymmetric downside, so the initial gate is intentionally strict and paired with a 98% review-routing recall threshold. The owner, Privacy, and clinical operations can revise it before launch if they document the risk rationale. Any revised threshold must still be frozen before evaluation so the team cannot move the goalposts after seeing results.
