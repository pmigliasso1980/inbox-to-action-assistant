# Kestrel’s administrative burden is concentrated in fragmented intake, status chasing, and denial feedback

## The engagement covered the highest-cost administrative teams but leaves central scheduling as a material blind spot

Kestrel Health Group operates **31 outpatient sites (reported)** with **218 clinicians (reported)** and **$412 million in annual net patient revenue (reported)**. Its cost to collect is **4.1% (reported)** versus a **3.2% peer benchmark (reported)**, accounts-receivable days increased from **41 to 46 (reported)**, and first-pass claim acceptance is below **88% (reported)**. The discovery therefore focused on Revenue Cycle, Practice Operations, People & Talent, and Patient Growth: **473 staff in scope (reported)**.

The evidence base combined **10 stakeholder interviews (measured from the dossier)**, **2 shadowing sessions totaling 255 minutes (measured)**, a survey with **153 responses from 473 invited staff, or 32% (measured)**, workflow documents, system inventories, and operating metrics. The survey was uneven: Patient Growth response was **74% (measured)** while Practice Operations response was **20% (measured)**. No Revenue Cycle work was shadowed, credentialing was interview-only, and central scheduling’s **70 staff (reported)** were not covered. Findings are therefore directional for those areas, not enterprise-wide conclusions.

## Work slows because staff bridge disconnected systems with spreadsheets, inboxes, phone calls, and memory

Three mapped workflows reveal the same operating pattern. In prior authorization, **14 coordinators (reported)** manage about **2,400 requests each month (reported)** through Caregate, payer portals, phone calls, and a coordinator-owned spreadsheet with **431 rows and 14 columns (observed)**. **61% of requests require a chase (reported)** and **18% are initially denied (reported)**. A shadowed phone chase took **21 minutes, including 13 minutes on hold (observed)**; **8 portal chases took 34 minutes (observed)**. New-request handling observed at **19, 14, and 17 minutes (observed)**, combined with the portal-chase lower bound, implies at least **771.7 administrative hours per month (projected)** before phone-chase time is fully counted.

Referral intake receives about **5,800 referrals per month (reported)**; **62%, or 3,596 referrals per month (projected)**, arrive by fax. **9 coordinators (reported)** inspect faxes, look up provider and routing information in an approximately **900-row spreadsheet (observed)**, enter data in Caregate, and seek missing information. Ten observed faxes imply a processing-time range of **6.3–8.1 minutes per fax (projected from the observed sample)**, or **377.6–485.5 hours per month (projected)** for fax intake. The sample is small and the true rework rate is unknown, so this is an addressable workload range, not promised savings.

Denials work begins after the revenue leak has already occurred. Kestrel submits about **47,000 claims per month (reported)** and has a **12.3% first-pass denial rate (reported)**, implying **5,781 denials per month (projected)**. Staff work **3,100 denials per month (measured)**, leaving a **2,681-case difference (projected)** that the dossier says is largely written off below a threshold. Missing or invalid authorization represents **34% of denials (reported)**, yet the appeal team’s roughly **1,400 SharePoint documents (observed)** have no usable precedent index. The reported staff throughput of **15–20 denials per person per day (reported)** implies **3,575–4,767 cases per month (projected)** for **11 staff (reported)**, which conflicts with the measured total and must be reconciled before benefits are claimed.

## Kestrel has enthusiastic users but weak controls, shared context, and production pathways

The weighted maturity result is **2.0 of 5.0 (assessed)**. Leadership and strategy score **2.5 (assessed)** because executives recognize the problem but have not named accountable workflow owners or baselines. People and skills score **2.0 (assessed)**: **51% of respondents report using AI for work (measured)**, **71% of those users rely on personal accounts (measured)**, but only **6 respondents report formal training (measured)**. Data scores **2.5 (assessed)** because useful operational data exists, but definitions, ownership, and lineage are fragmented.

Tools and technology score **2.0 (assessed)**. Caregate offers a read API with about **2 weeks of service-access lead time (reported)**, while write access requires vendor approval of **8–12 weeks (reported)**; RightFax exposes an API in about **1 day (reported)**. Governance and risk score **1.5 (assessed)** because no AI policy exists and Kestrel has no business-associate agreement with a general AI vendor. A PHI-bearing deployment requires a security and risk process with a minimum **6–8 week lead time (reported)**. Adoption and change also score **1.5 (assessed)**: staff are experimenting, but prior pilots lacked baselines and success metrics.

## The best near-term opportunities augment people and fix measurement before attempting autonomy

The first-ranked opportunity is a referral-intake extraction assistant. It would classify incoming documents, extract patient, referring-provider, specialty, and urgency fields, and route low-confidence or incomplete cases to a coordinator. The verdict is **AUGMENT** because humans must verify all fields and retain routing authority. The opportunity addresses **377.6–485.5 monthly hours (projected)**, has comparatively accessible RightFax data, and avoids Caregate write access during the pilot.

The second-ranked opportunity is a denial root-cause feedback dashboard. Its verdict is **GO — non-AI prerequisite**. A deterministic pipeline should first reconcile claim counts, worked denials, dispositions, reason codes, and write-offs; only then should an LLM summarize trends. This resolves the current **2,681-case monthly difference (projected)** and gives authorization, billing, and clinic leaders a shared causal view.

An appeal-precedent finder and prior-authorization chase digest are promising but follow later. The precedent finder needs document indexing, redaction, and access controls. The chase digest depends on unstable payer-portal access and uncertain authorization baselines. Credentialing should first receive a governed worklist. A patient-balance autonomous agent is **NO GO** because its harm, PHI, and collection risks exceed the quality of available controls.

## Two staged pilots can test value while preserving human accountability

**Pilot 1 — Referral-intake extraction assistant.** The baseline is **6.3–8.1 minutes per fax (projected)**, to be replaced by a **2-week measured baseline (proposed)** before release. The primary success metric is median verified handling time per fax; the proposed target is a **30% reduction (proposed)** after at least **400 production cases or 6 weeks (proposed)**. Secondary metrics are critical-field precision of at least **99.5% (proposed)** and human-review routing recall of at least **98% (proposed)**. The client-side owner should be the Practice Operations referral-intake director. The first action is to name that owner and approve a de-identified RightFax sample by **2026-10-16 (proposed)**.

**Pilot 2 — Denial root-cause feedback dashboard.** The baseline is **5,781 denials per month (projected)**, **3,100 worked denials per month (measured)**, and a **2,681-case difference (projected)**. The primary metric is the share of monthly denials reconciled to a disposition, with a proposed target of **at least 98% (proposed)** for **2 consecutive monthly closes (proposed)**. The secondary metric is time from month-end to a reviewed root-cause report, with the baseline to be measured before build. The client-side owner should be the Revenue Cycle director. The first action is a **90-minute data-definition workshop (proposed)** with Revenue Cycle, Finance, and IT by **2026-10-16 (proposed)**.

## A decision, named owners, and a compliant data path are now required

Kestrel should approve the two pilots in sequence, beginning with referral intake and the denial-data prerequisite in parallel. The executive sponsor should name the two client-side owners by **2026-10-16 (proposed)**. Security, Privacy, and IT should either approve a BAA-covered AI environment or explicitly restrict the referral pilot to local deterministic extraction by **2026-12-11 (projected target based on the reported 6–8 week review)**. The pilot team should not request Caregate write access until the evaluation thresholds are met; read-only and synthetic or de-identified operation are sufficient for the first gate.

The central decision is not whether Kestrel should “use AI.” It is whether it will govern and measure two bounded workflow changes whose failure modes are visible, reversible, and owned. Approval should be recorded by **2026-10-16 (proposed)** so the evidence-gathering and compliance work can begin.
