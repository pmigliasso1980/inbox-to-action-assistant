# Shadowing notes

## Prior authorization — 125 minutes observed

The coordinator began with 34 items due for chase. One phone chase took 21 minutes, including 13 minutes on hold. Eight portal rows were handled in 34 minutes. Three new requests took 19, 14, and 17 minutes. Clinic-information gaps ranged from 1 day to 1 week as reported during the session.

Invisible work included deciding which payer channel to use, interpreting historical notes, determining whether an attachment was sufficient, updating the local tracker, and translating payer status back to clinic language. The session supports a conservative portal-chase lower bound, but the phone sample is too small for a stable average.

## Referral intake — 130 minutes observed

Ten faxes were followed. Two clean examples took 5 minutes 20 seconds and 4 minutes 40 seconds. Six other clean examples took between 4 and 7 minutes. One problematic fax took 13 minutes and one missing-insurance fax took 16 minutes. The sample supports a projected average range of 6.3–8.1 minutes, depending on how the imprecise 4–7 minute observations are represented.

Invisible work included recognizing document type, searching a roughly 900-row provider file, resolving abbreviations, spotting missing fields, deciding who should be contacted, and selecting a destination. The coordinator’s judgment is not reducible to transcription; the pilot must preserve human routing authority.

## Observed versus stated differences

- Referral problems were 20% in the 10-case observation versus 25% reported; both remain uncertain because the sample is small.
- The prior-authorization observation confirms substantial waiting, but does not establish an enterprise phone-chase average.
- No Revenue Cycle or credentialing work was observed, so their maps explicitly retain interview-only assumptions.
