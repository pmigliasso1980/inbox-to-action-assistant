# Interview and shadowing critique

## What worked

The evidence captures concrete systems, volumes, exception paths, time observations, and operator workarounds rather than relying only on general opinions. Following real cases surfaced invisible judgment: provider lookup, channel selection, document sufficiency, and translation between payer and clinic language. Cross-checking interviews against survey and shadow data exposed contradictions instead of smoothing them away.

## What was weak

The sampling was uneven. Practice Operations survey response was 20%, central scheduling was not covered, Revenue Cycle was not shadowed, and credentialing was interview-only. The referral observation included only 10 cases, while the prior-authorization phone sample included only one case. Several important definitions were not resolved: denial population, “worked” disposition, authorization existed-but-not-attached frequency, and referral error rate.

## What the next round should ask

1. Pull two weeks of timestamped referral events stratified by document type and exception.
2. Reconcile claim, denial, worked, appealed, overturned, and written-off populations by service month.
3. Shadow Revenue Cycle for at least two queue cycles and credentialing across one complete payer submission.
4. Interview central scheduling and sample its handoffs with referral intake and clinics.
5. Ask operators to mark every time they leave the system of record and why.

## Ethical and recording note

The dossier does not establish recording consent, so these artifacts are synthesized notes rather than claimed transcripts. A real engagement should ask permission, stop recording immediately if anyone hesitates, and preserve only approved evidence.
